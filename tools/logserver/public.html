A pet door that opens for one animal.

An ESP32 listens for the Bluetooth beacon on your animal's collar. When it comes close, the door opens. When it has been gone a while, the door closes.

It wires into the motorised door you already have, by pressing its own buttons. No subscription, no vendor account, and about $80 in parts.

How it works

Four stages, each there to stop a different way of getting it wrong.

  1. Listen

    The radio passes on every advertisement from the collar, about 2–3 a second, not just the first one it hears.

  2. Filter

    A median removes the spikes reflections cause, then smoothing takes out the rest. Fast for opening, slow for closing.

  3. Decide

    Open above −65 dBm held for 1.5 s. Close below −75 dBm held for 15 s. A returning animal cancels a close.

  4. Press, then check

    A relay presses the door's own button. Optional sensors confirm it started and arrived; a close that stalls is reversed.

The door's own controller still runs the motor and stops it at its limits. PetDoor only ever presses a button, the same as you would. The original panel, its buttons and its timer all keep working.

Architecture

One board on the door does everything. The server is optional, and the door never waits for it.

Built so the door keeps working

Everything that matters runs on the door itself. With no WiFi, no server and no internet it opens and closes exactly the same, and keeps its own rolling log of the last 128 events in flash. WiFi can be compiled out entirely.

Add a server and the door uploads that log, signed with a key only you and the door know. Commands from the dashboard, such as open, close, lock, a setting or a firmware update, wait on the server until the door next calls in, and ride back in the reply. Credentials can never be changed remotely, and a firmware update that cannot reach the server afterwards rolls itself back.

Wiring

The conversion

Full steps, including which button pads to use and how to test before you solder, are in the build guide and wiring guide. The beacon guide explains why a consumer tracker will not work: an AirTag's typical gap between broadcasts is over five seconds, and it changes its address on purpose.

Safety

What the firmware will not do. Each of these is enforced in code, and most are tested.

  • Open on a stale signal.A lost or weak beacon can close the door, never open it.
  • Act before it has heard the beacon.A dead collar battery after a reboot cannot read as "gone" and shut the door.
  • Close in the first 30 s after power-up.A power blip must not bring the door down on an animal standing in it.
  • Close on a brief dropout.It needs 15 s of weak signal, and a returning animal cancels the close.
  • Retry a stalled close.It reverses and opens. Further tries are minutes apart and capped, then it stays open and says so.
  • Energise both relays together.The relays are interlocked, with a gap before every change of direction.
  • Press twice while the door is moving.On these controllers a second press means stop, which would park it halfway.
  • Stay in maintenance by accident.The window that keeps the beacon away from the motor ends by itself, and a reboot clears it.

Obstruction protection comes from the door, not from PetDoor. Buy a door whose own controller detects an obstruction if anything larger than a chicken will use it, and read the safety guide before you wire anything.

Dashboard

Optional, self-hosted, and behind your own sign-in.

One file of Python and a SQLite database, on a Raspberry Pi or a small VM. It turns months of the door's log into trips per day, how long each trip lasts and the daily rhythm. It also tracks the beacon battery and warns you weeks before the cell gives out. If you turn it on, it can drive the door too: open, close, lock, change a setting, or push new firmware. It can email you when anyone does.

The real log stays private because when a door opens and closes says when a house is empty. This page and the sample are the only public parts.

Before you build

The honest limits.

It is not a lock

Bluetooth advertisements are not authenticated. Anything that broadcasts your beacon's address opens the door, and a free phone app can read that address. That's fine against weather, rodents and the neighbour's cat, but it won't stop a person.

The animal has to wear it

No beacon, no door. That rules out an animal that won't keep a collar on. A flat cell stops the door opening; it never makes it close on an animal.

You are wiring a motor

PetDoor only presses buttons, and the door's controller keeps its limits. You are still working on a door an animal walks through.

You could just buy one

Finished smart pet doors exist, with a warranty and someone to call. If you want one working this afternoon, buy one. This project is for when you already own a powered door worth keeping, or want the log on hardware you control.
